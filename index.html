Part 1 of 4

FIRST
WORDS &
BASICS

First Word of Every Sentence
Capitalize the first word in every sentence. No exceptions.
EXAMPLE

The second Seminole War lasted nearly eight years.
After the war, many Seminoles remained in the Everglades.

First Word of a Line of Poetry
Traditionally, the first word in each line of a poem is capitalized.
MAYA ANGELOU

Storm, blow me from here
With your fiercest wind
Let me float across the sky
'Til I can rest again.

First Word of a Quoted Sentence
The first word of a quoted sentence begins with a capital letter, whether or not the quotation begins the sentence.
EXAMPLES

Hagrid says, "You're a wizard, Harry."
"If you have built castles in the air," Thoreau writes, "your work need not be lost."

HEADS UP: Capitalize only if the person you're quoting capitalized it, or it's the first word of the sentence.
What is suggested by the metaphor "castles in the air"? → lowercase, it's mid-sentence.
DIALOGUE FRAGMENT

When asked about Harry Potter, Jacob replied, "Only one book."
Fragments used in dialogue also get a capital first letter.

The Pronoun I and Interjection O
Always capitalize the pronoun I and the interjection O.
EXAMPLES
  • "Hear us, O Lord"
  • I finished the race, but oh was I exhausted.
  • The first line I read was "Hear us, O Zeus."
Note: The interjection oh is NOT capitalized unless it begins a sentence.
Salutation & Closing of a Letter
Capitalize the first word in both the salutation (greeting) and closing of a letter.

Salutations

  • Dear Mr. Lindeman,
  • My dear Jennifer,

Closings

  • Sincerely yours,
  • Yours truly,
Proper Nouns & Proper Adjectives
Capitalize proper nouns (specific names) and proper adjectives (adjectives formed from proper nouns).

Proper Noun

  • Dickens
  • France
  • the Midwest
  • Picasso, Lassie
  • Mr. Lindeman

Proper Adjective

  • Dickensian character
  • French bread
  • Midwestern values
  • Elizabethan times
Names of Persons & Animals
Capitalize the names of specific persons and animals.
EXAMPLES
  • Nolan Ryan  ·  Dr. Eileen Cruz  ·  Jerome Wilson, Jr.
  • Lassie  ·  Princess Leia  ·  Percy (Mr. Lindeman's lizard)
Geographical Names
Capitalize the names of continents, countries, cities, rivers, mountains, parks, highways, and other geographical features.

Capitalize

  • Europe · Argentina · Chicago
  • Galveston Island · Allegheny Mountains
  • Atlantic Ocean · Interstate 10
  • Fontainebleau State Park · Orleans Parish
  • the Northeast · Great Plains

Do NOT capitalize

  • directions:
  • "I am from the South." (region)
  • "Drive south to get there." (direction)

Organizations, Teams, Institutions & Government Bodies
Capitalize the full official names of organizations, sports teams, schools, and government agencies.
EXAMPLES
  • American Medical Association
  • New Orleans Saints  ·  Harlem Globetrotters
  • Mount Carmel Academy  ·  Florida State University
  • Department of the Interior  ·  New Orleans City Council
Buildings & Other Structures
Capitalize the names of specific buildings and structures.
EXAMPLES
  • Detroit Westin Hotel  ·  Fox Theater  ·  Colosseum
  • Metropolitan Museum  ·  Saenger Theater
  • Crescent City Connection
Monuments, Memorials & Awards
Capitalize the names of monuments, memorials, and awards.
EXAMPLES
  • Dinosaur National Monument  ·  Mount Rushmore
  • Korean War Veterans Memorial  ·  Lincoln Memorial
  • Nobel Prize  ·  Heisman Trophy  ·  Nobel Peace Prize
Businesses & Brand Names
Capitalize the names of businesses and the brand names of business products.
CAPITALIZE
  • Forever 21  ·  Apple Macintosh  ·  GMC Jimmy
Watch out: A common noun that follows a brand name and identifies the type of product is NOT capitalized.
→ Starbucks coffee  ·  Apple computer  ·  Schwinn bicycle
Historical Events, Periods, Holidays & Calendar Items
Capitalize historical events & periods, special events, holidays, months, and days of the week. Do not capitalize general time references.

Capitalize

  • Battle of Gettysburg · Dark Ages
  • Great Depression · Renaissance
  • Valentine's Day · Christmas
  • October · Friday · NBA All-Star Game

Do NOT capitalize

  • century · decade · era
  • "…during the fifth century"
Seasons
Do NOT capitalize the name of a season unless it is being personified or is part of a proper name.
EXAMPLES
  • We look forward to spring after the long winter. ← no cap
  • Autumn greeted us as a long-lost friend. ← personified = cap
  • Are you going to the Winter Formal dance? ← proper name = cap

PEOPLES,
PLANETS
& MORE

Nationalities & Peoples
Capitalize the names of nationalities, races, and ethnic groups.
EXAMPLES
  • Italian  ·  Hispanic  ·  Navajo  ·  Micronesian  ·  Cherokee
Religions, Holy Days & Sacred Writings
Capitalize names of religions and their followers, holy days and celebrations, sacred writings, and specific deities.

Capitalize

  • Judaism · Christianity · Hinduism
  • Ash Wednesday · Hanukkah
  • the Bible · the Koran · Dead Sea Scrolls
  • God · Jesus · the Holy Spirit

Exception

  • god and goddess are NOT capitalized when referring to deities of ancient mythology:
  • "The Roman goddess of grain was Ceres."
Ships, Trains, Aircraft & Spacecraft
Capitalize the names of specific ships, trains, aircraft, and spacecraft. The make and model of a vehicle are also capitalized.
EXAMPLES
  • Titanic  ·  Queen Mary  ·  Orient Express
  • Air Force One  ·  Apollo 11  ·  Atlantis
  • Kia Seltos  ·  Mitsubishi Eclipse  ·  GMC Jimmy
Planets, Stars & Heavenly Bodies
Capitalize the names of planets, stars, constellations, and other heavenly bodies.
CAPITALIZE
  • Neptune  ·  Mars  ·  Andromeda Galaxy  ·  Great Nebula  ·  Orion  ·  Milky Way
Two exceptions to know:
• earth is only capitalized when used with another capitalized heavenly body: "Unlike Earth, neither Mercury nor Venus has a moon."
• sun and moon are generally not capitalized: "The sun is a star. The moon is the earth's only natural satellite."
School Subjects
Do NOT capitalize school subjects, except for course names followed by a number and names of language classes.

Do NOT capitalize

  • algebra · chemistry · history
  • art · biology · math

DO capitalize

  • Algebra II · Chemistry I · Biology I
  • English · Spanish · Latin · French
Class names: Do not capitalize freshman, sophomore, junior, or senior unless part of a proper noun.
→ "A number of sophomores attended Junior Prom."  ·  "The Sophomore Singers performed."

TITLES
& FAMILY
WORDS

Person's Title Before a Name
Capitalize a person's title when it comes before the person's name.
CAPITALIZE
  • General Powell  ·  President Kennedy  ·  Archbishop Aymond
  • Governor Edwards  ·  Mrs. Ruckert
Title Alone or Following a Name
A title used alone or following a person's name is NOT capitalized, especially if preceded by a or the.
NOT CAPITALIZED
  • Who was the president during WWII?
  • She served as governor of New Jersey.
Heads up: A title used alone in direct address IS capitalized.
DIRECT ADDRESS
  • Well, Mayor, will you please test the microphone?
  • Do you intend to visit the disaster area, Governor?
Family Relationship Words
Capitalize a word showing family relationship when it is used before or in place of the person's name, unless the word follows a possessive noun or pronoun.

Capitalize

  • Aunt Brenda
  • Grandmother Bechtel
  • MawMaw Ruth
  • "Here comes Uncle Randy!"

Do NOT capitalize

  • My aunt Brenda
  • Maria's grandmother
  • Rory's aunt Katherine
  • "I asked my uncle."
Titles of Written Works
Capitalize the first and last words and all other important words in titles and subtitles. Do not capitalize articles, prepositions, or conjunctions unless they are the first word.
EXAMPLES
  • Harry Potter and the Sorcerer's Stone ← "and" + "the" not capped mid-title
  • Gone with the Wind ← "with" + "the" not capped
  • The Outsiders ← "The" IS capped (first word)
  • The Atlantic Monthly vs. the Rocky Mountain News ← "the" only capped if part of official title
